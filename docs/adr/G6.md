# G6. Aesthetic UI

**Status:** Accepted
**Design:** [visuals: the map is the game](../game-design.md#12-visuals-the-map-is-the-game), [onboarding and the Guide](../game-design.md#onboarding)
**Goals:** [plays like a game, easy to start, works everywhere, fast on ordinary hardware](../goals.md#player-oriented)

## Context

The game simulates a civilisation across the whole solar system, which is a lot of information to show: 25 bodies, 74 regions, six resources, fleets in transit, battles and other empires. If the interface shows all of it at once, the game becomes a spreadsheet, and the player stops feeling like they're at the head of a civilisation. The interface had to look good enough that the solar system itself is a reason to play, and stay clear enough that someone can tell what's going on without having played for long. It also had to run in a browser, on an ordinary laptop or a phone, for markers who arrive with no instructions.

## Options

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| A web app: pages, forms and tables, my first client [8b7f4ae](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-drvcarroll/commit/8b7f4ae) | Quick to build, easy to test, works on any device | Feels like admin software rather than a game, and hides the solar system behind lists |
| A dense HUD over the map, as in EU4 and Stellaris | Every number is one glance away | Overwhelming for a new player, and doesn't fit on a phone |
| The map as the whole screen, with a thin HUD and panels only for what's selected | The solar system stays in view and is the main interface, and a new player only sees what they've clicked on | Harder to build, and every action has to be reachable from something on the map |

## Decision

The full-screen 3D solar system is the main interface, in the style of Stellaris. The player zooms from the whole system down to a planet's surface and pans like on Google Maps, and does everything by clicking things on the map; clicking a planet, region or fleet opens a panel beside it with its actions, and the panel closes when they're done. There are no pages of forms, and no panel hides its contents behind tabs.

The map is the visual spectacle. Every playable body uses real imagery from NASA and USGS missions, Earth's regions are drawn with real country borders, a lit Sun casts real shadows, and a busy system of minor bodies sits around them. Fleets travel along their transfer arcs, armies stand on regions as icons with counts, and battles show on the region where they're happening, so a player can read the state of the world from the map.

The HUD over it is kept minimal: a top bar with each resource's icon, total and gain per day; an Empire menu with three dropdowns (Territory, Military and Research); a news ticker; and drawers for Empires, Chat and the Guide that open over the map. It's styled as a gunmetal command console, with engraved keys, a cyan highlight for anything live or selected, and monospace numbers, so it feels like a war table rather than a website. Resources and buildings are shown as named SVG icons, never as letters.

For a new player, there's a five-step tutorial checklist (build a Mine, research a tech, build a Spaceport, launch to Luna, claim a region) and an in-game Guide one click away from the top bar. I added both, together with the icons, after the crit 8 feedback said the game was addictive but players couldn't tell what to do next ([89b4079](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-drvcarroll/commit/89b4079)).

## Consequences

- A 3D client is far more work than pages of forms, and is the heaviest part of the code base. To keep it running on ordinary hardware, the client picks a graphics quality tier automatically, and textures stream in after the first view ([Compute Pushed to the Client](S1.md)).
- Every action has to be reachable from the map and also by keyboard alone, at both a desktop and a phone size, which means each new feature needs a map interaction, a keyboard path and a phone layout.
- With most information hidden until the player selects something, the interface has to tell them what they can do; every disabled button shows why, and every rejected action says what to do instead.
- Small layout changes can break the interface at one screen size without anyone noticing. A wider top-bar title once pushed the user menu off-screen and broke eight browser tests, so the browser tests now have to pass before any interface change is done ([fd07958](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-drvcarroll/commit/fd07958)).

## Evaluation

- **Enforced** ([client.test.ts](../../e2e/client.test.ts)): after joining, the map fills the screen; the wheel zooms and dragging pans; clicking a region opens its panel; the core loop (claim, build, launch, colonise) completes by keyboard alone at 1920×1080 and 390×844; every resource is shown by an icon with an accessible name; the Empire menu has exactly three dropdowns; a battle shows both sides' counts falling live; the first view loads in under 3 MB.
- **Measured** ([checks.md](../checks.md)): 60 fps on an integrated-graphics laptop at every quality tier, using under 300 MB of GPU memory, with the client stepping down to a lower tier on a slow GPU.
- **Judged**: the crit 8 playtest with classmates, whose feedback led to the icons, the tutorial and the Guide, and the LLM playtests, which rate whether players knew what to do next.
