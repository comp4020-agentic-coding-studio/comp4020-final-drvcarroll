# G4. Player vs Player Interaction

**Status:** Accepted
**Design:** [military and combat](../game-design.md#7-military), [trade, alliances and chat](../game-design.md#9-trade)
**Goals:** [every path is a real path, the frontier is real](../goals.md#player-oriented)

## Context

Resources are limited and spread unevenly ([A Complex Economy](G3.md)), so players run into each other, and what happens then is most of the game. I wanted those interactions to resemble how real nations deal with each other, through war, trade and alliances, and each option had to be worth choosing. War had to be strategic, which means a player can predict roughly how a battle will go before committing to it and can follow it while it happens. It also had to pay off, since a war that costs more than it gains will never be fought. Trade and alliances had to be just as rewarding, so a cooperative player isn't at a disadvantage against an aggressive one. And players needed a way to talk, since deals, pacts and threats all depend on it.

## Options

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Random combat (dice rolls, hit chances), as in Risk or Civilization | Upsets and drama | A player can't plan around a result they can't predict, and losing to a bad roll feels unfair |
| Unit counters (rock-paper-scissors), as in Age of Empires | Rewards scouting and army composition | Another system for a new player to learn, on top of the economy and orbits |
| Deterministic combat from Lanchester's square law | The outcome can be calculated in advance, and the maths is how real battles of attrition behave | No upsets, so the stronger side always wins a straight fight |
| War only, with trade as a side system | Simple | Cooperative players have no real path, and the game becomes a wargame |
| War, trade and alliances, each with a reward | Aggressive and cooperative players both have a reason to interact | More systems to balance against each other |

## Decision

Combat follows Lanchester's square law with no randomness. Each unit has a flat strength, and when two sides meet the stronger wins and keeps √(1 − (L/W)²) of its units, where L and W are the losing and winning strengths, so a player who knows both sides' strength knows the result. Space battles resolve instantly. Ground battles use the same equation but play out over time, about 21 seconds for 10 armies against 10, so both sides' counts fall on the map and either side can send reinforcements while the battle is still going. Defenders in their own region get +50% strength, so attacking needs a clear advantage. Wars are declared publicly and become active a minute later, which gives the target time to prepare.

War pays off directly. Taking a region transfers it with all its buildings, and the conqueror also takes 25% of the loser's stock of every resource, scaled by that region's share of the loser's territory. I added this plunder after the sim showed conquerors winning no stages at all, because a captured region alone didn't return what the war cost.

Trade pays off as well. Players offer goods for goods, both sides go into escrow when the offer is accepted, and Freighters deliver them. If either nation keeps an Envoy at the other's capital, both deliveries are multiplied by up to 1.9×, depending on the Science research of the nation keeping the Envoy, so a trade between two allied research nations is worth almost double what either side put in. An alliance of up to four nations forces peace between its members, fights as one side against a shared enemy (fleets combine in orbit and armies join each other's attacks and defences), and lets any member place sanctions that block all trade between the alliance and a rival.

Players talk through chat, with a global channel, an alliance channel and direct messages. Direct messages stay open between nations at war or under sanctions, since that's when players most need diplomacy.

## Consequences

- With no randomness, the weaker side can't win a straight fight, so a weaker player has to find other ways to win, such as the defender's bonus, allies, timing an attack while the enemy's fleet is away, or picking a different path to expansion.
- A player can only see what they hold or pay an Envoy to watch, so planning a war usually starts with gathering information first.
- Plunder makes conquest profitable, which also makes the weakest players attractive targets. The sim showed late joiners falling further behind after plunder was added, which led to new player protection increasing by game length ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
- Chat sits outside the game rules, so it isn't replayed from the command log and an admin reset clears it.

## Evaluation

- **Enforced** ([war.test.ts](../../rules/war.test.ts)): survivors match the square law exactly and equal sides annihilate; a 10 against 10 ground battle lasts about 21 seconds and ends with the same survivors as the instant result; the garrison bonus holds; reinforcements extend a battle; conquest moves the plundered share of stocks.
- **Enforced** ([alliance.test.ts](../../rules/alliance.test.ts), [trade.test.ts](../../rules/trade.test.ts)): two allied fleets beat one that neither could beat alone; sanctions block offers, accepts and gifts both ways; the Envoy multiplier scales both deliveries.
- **Enforced** ([game.test.ts](../../spec/game.test.ts), [client.test.ts](../../e2e/client.test.ts)): chat reaches every allowed client within a second and survives a reconnect, and direct messages never reach anyone outside them; in the browser, a player declares war, attacks, and watches both sides' army counts fall live.
- **Simulated** ([balance.test.ts](../../sim/balance.test.ts)): the conqueror bot now wins 10% of stages, up from none before plunder ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
- **Judged** (`/playtest`): in the third LLM playtest, six players made 2 wars, 4 attacks, 7 trades and 1 alliance in 21 minutes, and both cooperative and aggressive players placed in the top three ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
