# G1. Military, Economic, and Technological Growth

**Status:** Accepted
**Design:** [the idea](../game-design.md#1-the-idea), [stages and scoring](../game-design.md#10-victory-stages-in-a-world-that-keeps-going)
**Goals:** [every path is a real path, well paced](../goals.md#player-oriented)

## Context

The driving idea of the game is to expand, but "expand" on its own isn't something a player can aim at, or something the server can score, so it had to be decomposed into concrete goals. Those goals needed to accommodate very different players and playstyles (the conqueror, the trader, the builder, the researcher), feel connected to the real world rather than an abstract points race, and stay simple enough that a new player picks them up in their first few minutes.

## Options

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| A single goal, like territory held (Risk) | Easy to learn and to score | Only really suits one playstyle; a trader or researcher has to convert their advantage into land before it counts for anything |
| Many goals (conquer, trade, research, cooperate, etc), my first attempt [6325c60](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-drvcarroll/commit/6325c60) | Every playstyle gets its own way to win | Trade and cooperation were too hard to measure clearly, and with that many goals they can't compose with each other or be ranked against one another in a single stage |
| Military, economic and technological growth | Grounded in how societies have actually grown and competed, suits different playstyles, and few enough to combine | The balance between the three needs constant tuning |
| No score at all (a sandbox) | No balance problem | No stakes, and nothing for the stages ([Endless play](G5.md)) to rank |

## Decision

Expansion is decomposed into military, economic and technological growth, scored as **Territory** (5 per region, +20 per whole body), **Economy** (1 per 5 production per minute) and **Tech** (6 × rung number per rung), which sum into one score shown live in the Empires overlay.

These three come from real historic and geopolitical observations of how societies grow and compete; the great powers of history rose on some mix of military reach, economic weight and technological edge, and usually lost their place when one of the three fell behind. Building the score on that does a lot for the realism, and makes the game feel connected to the real world. They're also attached to things players probably strive for in their own lives (power, wealth, knowledge), which I think is a big part of what makes this kind of game addictive; you're chasing something you already want.

Three is simple enough to learn quickly, but there's still enough composition between them to be interesting, since a trader can fund an army, a researcher can unlock better production, and a conqueror takes land that produces more, without the goals getting too complex to reason about. Military itself is scored only through the territory it takes, so a fleet that never takes ground is worth nothing and nobody wins by stockpiling armies, and trade only counts once it's turned into production.

## Consequences

- Each path has to be kept roughly as strong as the others, which is ongoing work logged in [balance-log.md](../balance-log.md). Researchers won 1% of stages until tech scoring went up sixfold, and conquerors won nothing until conquest started carrying off a quarter of the loser's stocks ([41f93a7](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-drvcarroll/commit/41f93a7)).
- Mixed play is rewarded, since the three feed each other; the strongest nations in playtests pursued more than one.

## Evaluation

- **Simulated** ([balance.test.ts](../../sim/balance.test.ts)): six bot strategies each win 10 to 35% of stages over 24 seeded 60-minute seasons. Latest run: expander 17%, builder 29%, trader 39%, conqueror 10%, researcher 6%. Trader and researcher are still outside the target ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
- **Simulated**: no nation sweeps the first three stages in more than half of runs (8 of 24), and the leader holds 30% of total score at 30 minutes, under the 40% snowball limit.
- **Judged** (`/playtest`): six LLM players on the three paths, cooperative and aggressive. In the third playtest Tech players finished 2nd and 3rd, and the spread between paths was the tightest of the three runs ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
