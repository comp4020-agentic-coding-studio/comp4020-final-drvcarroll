# G5. Endless Play

**Status:** Accepted
**Design:** [time model](../game-design.md#3-time-model-analytic), [stages](../game-design.md#10-victory-stages-in-a-world-that-keeps-going), [players and presence](../game-design.md#11-players-and-presence)
**Goals:** [well paced, never lose progress](../goals.md#player-oriented)

## Context

Civilisation doesn't stop and restart every hour, and the game is meant to feel like steering one through the long run, so I didn't want a fixed match length. The game is built for a group of friends who drop in and out over anything from ten minutes to ten weeks, which raises three problems. First, a world with no end needs some structure, or there's nothing to aim for and the leader just keeps getting further ahead. Second, a player who joins a week late has to be able to compete with nations that have been growing all that time. Third, the world has to keep going and keep everything players have done while nobody is online, on a server that shuts down when it's idle.

## Options

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-length matches, as in most RTS games | Clear start and end, and everyone starts equal | Each match throws away the world players built, and a group can't play casually over days |
| One endless world with a running score | Persistent, and the world keeps what players built | The early leader snowballs, and a late joiner has no chance of catching up |
| Seasons that reset the world every few hours or weeks | Regular fresh starts | Still throws everything away, just less often |
| One endless world, ranked in stages by score gained | Persistent, with regular goals, and a small or late empire can win a stage | Eventually all regions are claimed, so the game stalls as a function of map size |

## Decision

The world never ends on its own; only an admin reset clears it. Its time is split into 20-minute stages. Each stage ranks nations by the score they gained during it, not the score they hold, so growth wins a stage rather than size, and a late joiner or a small empire can top one. The top three go into a hall of fame, and the news ticker announces the winner and the next stage.

Each new stage adds a global modifier for everyone, and the modifiers stack, roughly following the ages of history: an Industrial Age boosts Mines and Refineries, a Space Age makes Spaceports and ships cheaper and faster, then an Information Age, an Expansion Age and a Fusion Age. A world that has been running for two hours plays differently from one that just started, and each age shifts which strategy is strongest, so the leader from the last stage has to adapt.

Late joiners get extra help. Anyone joining more than five minutes in gets double production for their first five minutes, every new or eliminated nation starts on a free Earth region with a minute of protection from attack, and eliminated nations respawn the same way. Joining is always open while any Earth region is free.

The world keeps running with nobody online. The game state is stored as stocks, rates of change and a queue of scheduled events (a building finishing, a fleet arriving, a stage ending), rather than being updated by a loop every frame. When anyone reconnects, the server works out the current state exactly, from the last known state and the time that has passed, so a fleet launched before everyone logged off has arrived by the time they log back on. Every accepted command is stored in order before any player sees its result ([Persistent, Traceable Execution](S3.md)), so nothing a player did is lost across restarts or redeploys.

## Consequences

- Ranking by gain rather than total means a large empire can lose a stage to a smaller one that grew faster, which may feel unfair to the leader, though their total score is still shown live in Empires.
- The modifiers stop changing after the sixth stage, so a world that runs for days settles into the same rules after two hours.
- Catch-up help for late joiners isn't enough yet. In the latest sim, a nation joining 30 minutes in reaches 15% of the median score by the hour mark, against a target of 25%, mostly because conquerors pick on the weakest nation since plunder was added ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
- Computing state from time rather than running a loop means every rule has to be written as a rate or a scheduled event, which made rules like ground battles harder to write, since their timing had to be solved exactly rather than stepped.

## Evaluation

- **Enforced** ([economy.test.ts](../../rules/economy.test.ts)): stages advance every 20 minutes, rank by gain, and never end; a late joiner can top a stage; each stage's modifier applies to everyone; the state is identical whether the world advances every tick or only between commands.
- **Enforced** ([server.test.ts](../../server/server.test.ts), [game.test.ts](../../spec/game.test.ts)): the world keeps going while nobody is connected and catches up its stages; state survives a restart; a command is still there after a fresh login; an admin reset empties the world but keeps accounts.
- **Simulated** ([balance.test.ts](../../sim/balance.test.ts)): no nation sweeps the first three stages in more than half of runs (8 of 24), the leader holds 30% of total score at 30 minutes (limit 40%), and a respawned nation still holds a region ten minutes later. The late joiner target is not yet met ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
- **Judged** ([checks.md](../checks.md)): a next-day return pass, checking that a player coming back after the server has stopped sees their empire, the past stages and their results.
