# G2. The Real Solar System on a Smaller Scale

**Status:** Accepted
**Design:** [pace and orbits](../game-design.md#2-pace-and-orbits), [the map](../game-design.md#4-map), [travel](../game-design.md#travel)
**Goals:** [distance and time are a real cost, the frontier is real, well paced](../goals.md#player-oriented)

## Context

The game is meant to make the player feel like they're steering a civilisation out into space, and that only lands if the space feels real. The space-based grand strategy games I took inspiration from use invented galaxies and treat a planet as a single tile, while travel is a fixed number of turns down a hyperlane. That's fine for them, but it throws away two things I wanted; the sense that you're expanding into the actual solar system, and the strategy that comes from distance and time actually costing something. The catch is that the real solar system runs on a scale of years and decades, and a session has to fit into an evening, so it had to be shrunk without losing what makes it real.

## Options

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| An invented map (procedural galaxy, hyperlanes) | Free to design for balance, every map different | Loses the familiarity and the tangibility; conquering "Planet 7" means nothing, conquering Mars does |
| The real solar system, but static (fixed distances, travel in fixed time) | Real names and imagery, easy to balance | Distance becomes a flat number, so there's no timing, and no planning around windows |
| The real solar system at real speed | As authentic as it gets | A trip to Mars takes months, so nothing happens within a session |
| The real solar system, compressed in time but with real orbits | Real bodies, real regions and real orbital mechanics, with the timing and planning that comes with them, at a pace that fits a session | Real yields and distances aren't balanced by default, so the numbers need tuning |

## Decision

The map is the real solar system: 25 bodies and 74 regions. Earth's regions are groups of real countries drawn with their real borders, and every off-Earth region is a real named feature (Hellas is the Hellas basin, Olympus sits on Olympus Mons), placed from the USGS planetary nomenclature. Bodies orbit at their real periods and distances, and travel between them follows a Hohmann transfer, the same minimum-energy orbit real missions fly.

Time is compressed so one game day is one real second. A year takes about six minutes, Mars goes around the Sun in 11.5 minutes, and an Earth to Mars transfer window opens every 13 minutes instead of every 26 months. Launching outside the window costs up to three times the energy, and fleets in transit can't be turned around, so a launch is a real commitment.

This does a few things at once. Real bodies and regions immerse the player in a world they already know, which makes the achievements feel tangible and satisfying; taking Mars means something that taking an invented planet doesn't. The solar system is also a visual spectacle in its own right, with every body wearing real imagery from NASA and USGS missions. And because positions keep moving, time and space become real constraints; a fleet six minutes out is a live tension, Mars is closer some minutes than others, and outer bodies are expensive to reach and slow to reinforce, so expansion has to be planned rather than clicked.

Resources follow the geography as well. Each region has its own mix of Metals, Volatiles and Solar (Psyche is a metal world, Europa and Titan are rich in ice, Solar falls off with the square of the distance from the Sun), so nobody can be self-sufficient from one place, which pushes nations outward and into contact with each other ([A Complex Economy](G3.md)). Earth itself is the one exception; every start region sums to the same total, so where you start is flavour, not advantage.

## Consequences

- Real distances and yields aren't balanced by default, so the pacing had to be tuned around them. Early on, no bot ever reached a colony at all, because the first ship was out of reach; it took changes to the starting kit, Spaceport build time and Foundry output before the first colony landed in the target window ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
- Every zone is reachable from the start, with better drives reducing the cost rather than unlocking destinations, so distance is a cost curve rather than a wall.
- Positions are computed from time rather than stored, so the whole solar system costs nothing to persist and moves identically on the server and every client.
- Some realism had to give for clarity; orbits are circular and on one plane, and moons share their planet's position for travel.

## Evaluation

- **Enforced** ([map.test.ts](../../rules/data/map.test.ts)): 25 bodies and 74 regions; every off-Earth region centres on its real feature; every Earth region holds its real countries, none twice; real cities land in the right region (Paris in Western Europe, Novosibirsk in Siberia); Solar falls off as 1/d²; every start region sums to the same total.
- **Enforced** ([fleets.test.ts](../../rules/fleets.test.ts)): Earth to Mars takes the Hohmann time; the window penalty runs from 1× in the window to 3× at worst; contested regions go to the first arrival.
- **Simulated** ([balance.test.ts](../../sim/balance.test.ts)): the first off-Earth colony lands at 7.5 minutes (target 5 to 8, never before 5) and the first Mars colony at 22.5 minutes (target 12 to 25), so distance paces expansion without stalling it ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
- **Judged** ([checks.md](../checks.md)): timed stranger runs at each crit, recording whether a new player finds and completes claim, build, launch and colonise from the map alone.
