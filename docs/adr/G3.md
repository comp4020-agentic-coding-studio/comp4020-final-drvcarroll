# G3. A Complex Economy

**Status:** Accepted
**Design:** [resources](../game-design.md#5-resources-6), [buildings](../game-design.md#6-buildings-7), [military units](../game-design.md#7-military), [the map](../game-design.md#4-map)
**Goals:** [every path is a real path, well paced, self-explaining](../goals.md#player-oriented)

## Context

Every path to expansion runs through the economy. A conqueror needs armies, a trader needs goods to sell, a researcher needs labs, and everyone needs ships to get off Earth, so the economy is what all three playstyles are balanced against. It had to be complex enough to feel realistic and give each playstyle something to manage, but in a real-time game the player can't pause, so managing production and trade should take skill without becoming a full-time job. It also had to gate progression, so players earn colonies and warships over time, and it had to spread resources unevenly across the map, so nations have a reason to trade or fight rather than stay at home.

## Options

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| One currency (gold, credits) | Easy to learn and to balance | No supply chains, so there's nothing to trade and nothing worth fighting over, and every region is interchangeable |
| A deep economy with a dozen or more goods, as in Victoria or Anno | Very realistic, with rich trade | Too much to manage in real time, and a new player can't follow it in their first few minutes |
| A minimal production chain, where each resource exists because a mechanic needs it | Each resource has an obvious purpose, and the chain is short enough to remember | Fewer variables, so each one has a bigger effect when balancing |

## Decision

The economy has six resources and seven buildings, and I cut each down to the minimum a mechanic needs. I designed the chain backwards from the mechanics. War needs armies, and armies cost Materiel, which Factories make. Factories take Alloys and Volatiles, Alloys come from a Foundry that takes Metals, and every building uses Energy. Research is separate and pays for tech. That gives six resources (Energy, Metals, Volatiles, Alloys, Materiel and Research) and six buildings that make them, each building making exactly one resource. The seventh building is the Spaceport, which builds ships and armies.

Costs go up the further along the chain an item is. Basic buildings cost Metals, advanced buildings also cost Alloys, and every building costs Energy upkeep. Armies cost Materiel, ships cost Alloys and Materiel, launches cost Energy depending on distance, and each colony costs one Materiel per minute, with its output halved if the player can't pay. A Spaceport and Colony Ship together cost slightly more Alloys than the starting kit has, so the player needs some Foundry output or a trade before their first colony. Warships cost more at each tier but give more strength per resource spent, so late-game fleets have fewer, stronger ships.

Resources are spread unevenly across the map. Each region has its own mix of Metals, Volatiles and Solar; Psyche is almost pure metal, Europa and Titan are mostly ice, and Mercury gets six times Earth's sunlight. Regions also have few building slots (four on Earth, two or three elsewhere), so one region can't produce everything, and no nation can supply itself from home for long. Players have to expand to get what they're missing, and trade, ally or go to war with whoever holds it. Two players alone in the world can still trade with the Earth Exchange at a flat 3:1, so a missing resource is expensive to get but never impossible.

## Consequences

- With a chain this long, any one shortage stalls everything after it, and most early balancing went into fixing that. In the first sim runs the early game had too few Alloys, so no bot could afford a colony from the starting kit; Materiel ran out by minute three and slowed every Lab; and conquerors couldn't train armies without a Materiel income, so I gave every capital a small Materiel output of its own. Each fix changed a single number, followed by a rerun of the sim ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
- When a stock reaches zero, every building that consumes it runs at supply divided by demand, so the nation slows down rather than stalls.
- Since each building makes one resource and costs follow the chain, a shortage always traces back to one building, so when the server rejects an action it can tell the player which building to build.
- All the numbers sit in one data folder (`rules/data/`), so I tune the economy by editing data, never by changing the rules.

## Evaluation

- **Enforced** ([economy.test.ts](../../rules/economy.test.ts)): the starting kit matches a hand calculation; stocks never go negative or over their cap; consumers slow to supply over demand when a stock runs out; rejecting an action for a missing resource names the building that makes it.
- **Enforced** ([tables.test.ts](../../rules/data/tables.test.ts)): warship strength per resource rises with each tier.
- **Simulated** ([balance.test.ts](../../sim/balance.test.ts)): dead time in the first 30 minutes is 4% (target under 20%), the first colony lands at 7.5 minutes, and every strategy wins a share of stages ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
- **Judged** (`/playtest`): LLM players rated whether they knew what to do next and whether their choices mattered. When several players got confused about the same resource problem, such as replacing the only building that made something, I added a warning to the interface ([balance-log.md](../balance-log.md)).
