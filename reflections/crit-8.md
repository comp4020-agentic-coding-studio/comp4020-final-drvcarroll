# Crit-8 Reflection

The breakthrough that moved the work forward was that LLMs are getting really good at software development, and trying to build a super fine-grained restrictive harness does not provide the best output, as it strangles the LLMs post-training behaviour geared towards design freedom and long horizon tasks. Instead, it's faster to iteratively prototype (with some pretty strict guardrailes nonetheless, don't get me wrong) to maintain alignment, and get a more polished result with less initial work. There is a balance between letting the LLM do the heavy lifitng, and ensuring sufficient guardrailes are in place to allow it to thrive, without completely losing the plot.

This made me realise that software development as we know it is dying very quickly; if i am to survive, i need to adapt and evolve with the times. My next tattoo: "there will be no war with the machines - only a monthly subscription." Obviously my degree isnt useless; computer science is largely theoretical, and not purely an applied field. Building robust systems, solving difficult problems, understanding what design decisions need to be made and why, these are the things that will persist and carry me through the future.
